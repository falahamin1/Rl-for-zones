\paragraph{Running example.}
\textbf{PJS\_03} (Figure~\ref{fig:pjs03_pta}) is our running example: three
jobs $J_1, J_2, J_3$ share two machines $m_1$ and $m_2$, each job consisting
of two tasks that must execute in order.  $J_1$ runs $T_{1,1}$ on $m_1$
(duration $1$, $4$, or $8$ with probabilities $0.4$/$0.3$/$0.3$) followed by
$T_{1,2}$ on $m_2$ ($2$/$5$/$8$; $0.3$/$0.4$/$0.3$).  $J_2$ crosses the
other way: $T_{2,1}$ on $m_2$ ($1$/$3$/$6$; $0.3$/$0.4$/$0.3$) then
$T_{2,2}$ on $m_1$ ($2$/$5$/$8$; $0.3$/$0.4$/$0.3$).  $J_3$ mirrors $J_1$:
$T_{3,1}$ on $m_1$ ($2$/$4$/$7$; $0.4$/$0.3$/$0.3$) then $T_{3,2}$ on $m_2$
($1$/$4$/$7$; $0.3$/$0.4$/$0.3$).  The full system PTA is the synchronous
composition of the three per-job automata under the machine-exclusion
constraint that each machine processes at most one task at a time; clocks
$x_1, x_2, x_3$ track elapsed time per job and are reset to zero on each
dispatch.

\textit{Scheduling conflict.}
At $t{=}0$, tasks $T_{1,1}$ ($m_1$) and $T_{3,1}$ ($m_1$) are simultaneously
enabled but share machine $m_1$, so the scheduler must choose which to
dispatch first; $T_{2,1}$ ($m_2$) can start immediately in either case.
High duration variance (e.g.\ $T_{1,1}$ takes $1$, $4$, or $8$ time units)
makes the optimal decision non-trivial.

\textit{State-space contrast.}
The \emph{region-based} representation discretises each clock $x_i$ to an
integer in $\{0,\ldots,c_i{+}1\}$, where $c_i$ is the maximum task duration
for job $i$ and $c_i{+}1$ is a single absorbing value for ``above the
maximum.''  The number of distinct clock tuples is therefore bounded by
\[
  \prod_{i=1}^{n}(c_i + 2),
\]
which is exponential in the number of jobs $n$.  For PJS\_03,
$c_1{=}c_2{=}8$ and $c_3{=}7$, giving at most $10\times10\times9 = 900$
clock combinations.  The \emph{zone-based} representation instead groups all
clock valuations that are indistinguishable for scheduling into a single DBM,
keeping the symbolic state space far smaller in practice.
